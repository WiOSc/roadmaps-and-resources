# 🎬 The Video Editing Roadmap (that nobody made, so i made it)

> there's no official ONE PATH TO BECOME GAWX (follow this guy if you dont already) to video editing. so this is just how _i_ would look at things if i wanted to actually get better, without wasting months on stuff that doesn't matter 😎

## Prerequisites

### 🧭 First, what even is "video editing"?

"video editing" is not ONE thing. it's like saying "i do food". okay but like... what kind of food 😭

there are a bunch of different types of videos:

- **SaaS / motion graphics videos** → lots of animations and designing. clean, slick, product-y stuff
- **vlog style / talking head content** → you talk to camera, you cut out the boring parts, you keep the juicy parts
- **cinematic videos** → you're telling a story with **audio AND video** (visual storytelling, big movie energy 🍿)
- **and so much more but im too lazy to write them down here, but im sure from the above points you must've gotten the idea**

i'm NOT saying pick one right now. just have a rough idea of what field is what. later when you realise "oh wait THIS is what i'm into", you can go deep into that one.

### 🚫 Do NOT start with learning a software

yes, software matters. i'm not saying skip it. but if you learn the software **along with** building your creative library (step 1), you will improve MUCH faster than doing software first.

### What you actually need

- a phone or a laptop
- a free editor (that's enough to start, gear is not the bottleneck)
- curiosity and eyes that actually pay attention 👀

## Learning Path

### 1. 📚 Build your creative library (aka "taste")

> to make something cool you should be **FAMILIAR** with what cool things are.

no one, not even van gogh or any artist you look up to, made what they made just out of thin air. they looked at SO much art and thought about art so much that they could take bits from every experience they had and mash them together into something new.

and whenever they came across something, they'd ask:

**"what about this makes me feel what i'm feeling?"**

and that just goes into the brain library. like what makes a person feel what.

so let's say you have to make a **sad video**. now you already know which sounds, which colours, which elements make a viewer feel sad, because you've been collecting that info for ages 🧠

and yeah, no art in this world is truly "original". everything is a reference of something that's already there. that's not a bad thing, that's literally how creativity works.

this library = **taste** (some people call it that). it's what lets you make something new.

you make, you are, what you consume. so if you want to be like somebody or create like somebody start consuming that person's content, start observing what about their content makes you feel what you feel and try to replicate one effect one by one in your content.

#### Learn

- **Consume, but like, actually pay attention.** creating is half of it, consuming is the other half. not lazy scroll-and-forget consuming
- when you think _"wow that was so cool"_ or _"damn that was motivating"_: pause it, **rewind it**, figure out **WHY** you felt that way
- what was the music doing? the cuts? the colours? the zoom? remember it (or write it down)
- **Keep a swipe file.** make a folder / notion page / saved playlist for stuff that hits you. clips, transitions, color palettes, sound effects. this is your creative library in physical form

being super attentive and observant is very necessary. basically be a detective but for vibes 🕵️

#### Build

a swipe file with at least 20 things you found cool, each with a one line note on **why** it hit you.

#### You should learn

- how to analyze what makes you feel what you feel
- how to tell what kind of video is what (SaaS motion, vlog, cinematic)
- that nothing is "original", everything is remixed (and that's fine)

---

### 2. 💻 Learn the software (by recreating, not by tutorials)

here's the secret: **you don't need to watch youtube tutorials.**

the best way to learn an editing software:

1. pick a youtube video / insta reel / movie scene you think is super cool
2. try to **recreate it**
3. get stuck → google _"how to do xyz effect in xyz software"_
4. fix it, move on, repeat

you'll learn your way around the software naturally. compare that to watching a 1 hour tutorial and then sitting down to make your own video and remembering like... 3 things ;-;

**taste + a grip on the software** = those are the only fundamentals you need.

#### Learn

- pick any editor, a free one is totally fine (DaVinci Resolve, CapCut, whatever you vibe with)
- **which software tho?** when you're just starting out, **ANY software is good.** don't stress about it, the skills (taste, pacing, storytelling) carry over everywhere. but when you're getting ready to enter the **big leagues** (clients, jobs, bigger projects, working with other editors), switch to the **industry standard** stuff like the **Adobe suite** (Premiere Pro, After Effects), **DaVinci Resolve** or **Final Cut Pro**. that's what studios and teams actually use, so you wanna be comfy in it 😎
- google every effect you get stuck on, one problem at a time
- **learn keyboard shortcuts.** the faster your hands, the less your brain gets interrupted, and the more you can actually think about the creative stuff

#### Build

recreate **one** clip you love (a reel, a youtube intro, a movie scene) as close to the original as you can.

#### You should learn

- the timeline, cutting, layering, keyframes
- basic effects and transitions
- how to export properly
- beginner: any software works. going pro: move to an industry standard (Adobe suite, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro)

---

### 3. 📖 Storytelling

#### Learn

- how to write a script that's not JUST a script, but an actual great story
- watch how stories you love are structured (hook → build up → payoff)

#### Build

a 30-60 second story with a clear beginning, middle and end.

#### You should learn

- hooks and structure
- how to show instead of tell
- how audio AS WELL as video tells the story

---

### 4. 🖼️ Composition

#### Learn

- how to frame stuff so it looks good
- study frames from movies and videos you like, pause and look at where everything is placed

#### Build

shoot 10 shots of the same subject with 10 different framings.

#### You should learn

- rule of thirds, leading lines, depth
- why some shots feel balanced and others feel tense

---

### 5. 💡 Lighting

#### Learn

- how to set up good lighting for videos
- how light changes the mood of the same scene

#### Build

film the same subject in 3 different lighting setups (window light, lamp, backlit) and compare.

#### You should learn

- soft vs hard light
- key light, fill light, backlight
- how lighting connects to mood

---

### 6. ⚡ Pacing (MUST HAVE)

#### Learn

- knowing where to cut and at what speed things flow
- re-watch your favorite edits and count how long each shot stays on screen
- **cut more than you think you should.** if you're wondering "should i cut this?"... yeah, probably

#### Build

edit the same footage twice, once fast and once slow, and see how the feeling changes.

#### You should learn

- cutting on action and on beats
- rhythm, breathing room, speed ramps
- when to hold a shot and when to let go

---

### 7. 🎧 Music and sound

#### Learn

- picking music based on what you want the viewer to FEEL
- **sound is half the video.** good audio with okay video > great video with bad audio. every time. learn sound design early, it's the cheat code

#### Build

take one silent clip and score it 3 different ways (happy, sad, tense).

#### You should learn

- syncing cuts to music
- sound effects and layering
- audio mixing basics (levels, clean voice, background music volume)

---

### 8. 🎨 Color grading

#### Learn

- making it all look the way it feels
- study how your favorite films and videos use colour

#### Build

grade one clip in 3 moods (warm and cozy, cold and sad, high contrast and moody).

#### You should learn

- color correction vs color grading
- LUTs and how to use them without overdoing it
- how colour shifts emotion

---

### 9. 📁 Workflow: stay organized and efficient

#### Learn

- from the very beginning: **every project gets its own folder.** all the files for that project live in there
- when you start working on bigger projects, this will save your life. trust me. nobody wants to hunt for `final_final_v3_REAL.mp4` across 6 different folders 😭
- **try to be efficient.** many people can edit videos, but if you can edit them faster, that's what you want. when you make an effect dont feel good about it and leave it there, save it as a preset, so that next time you just have to drag and drop that when you need something similar
- **back up your work.** drive crashes are a real thing and they love ruining people's weeks

something like:

```
my-project/
├── footage/
├── audio/
├── graphics/
├── exports/
└── project-file
```

#### Build

a project folder template you reuse for every video, plus a small preset library of your favorite effects.

#### You should learn

- folder structure and naming
- saving and reusing presets
- backing up

---

## Projects

after all this, the only thing that makes you better is **making stuff**. watch as many videos as you can, recreate everything you find cool, repeat forever. that's literally the loop 🔁

### Beginner

- recreate a reel or youtube intro you love
- a 30 second video with only 5 clips (limits force creativity)
- a short clip scored 3 different ways

### Intermediate

- a vlog style video with proper cuts, music and color
- a short cinematic scene with a story, planned lighting and composition
- recreate a movie scene shot for shot

### Advanced

- a full SaaS style motion graphics promo
- a short film with script, shot list, sound design and grade
- a video in your chosen field that you'd be proud to put in a portfolio

## Additional Resources

- follow **GAWX** if you dont already
- your own swipe file (the best resource there is)
- free editors: DaVinci Resolve, CapCut and more
- free music and sound effects sites (look up royalty free libraries)

### 🎁 Bonus tips

- **Finish your projects.** an okay video that's done teaches you more than a "perfect" one that sits at 80% forever
- **The first 3 seconds matter.** whatever the platform, hooks are everything. study how videos you love open
- **Get feedback.** show your stuff to people (ideally people who'll be honest). you stop being able to see your own video clearly after the 50th watch
- **Don't buy gear to feel productive.** a phone and a free editor are enough to start. gear is not the bottleneck, practice is
- **Use limits on purpose.** "make a 30 sec video with only 5 clips" is a great way to force creativity
